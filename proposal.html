A proposal for Susan de Menil

A historic conference on antiquities as civilizational memory, and how technology can secure the future of the past.

AABC sits at one of the most resonant problems in culture today. A landmark convening can elevate the consortium's stature, advance its working agenda, and bring the technology community into productive contact with a field in need of next‑gen solutions.

Prepared by Andrew Goldstein
Dedalus Media Architects · May 2026

The Challenge

The infrastructure for preserving civilizational memory is broken.

Antiquities and heritage objects are the physical record of humanity's shared history.

But our broken systems for tracking these objects have resulted in a miasma of forgeries, illicit trafficking, and misattribution.

The result: Institutions are wary of engaging, markets cannot function with confidence, and scholarship has a fragmented picture of the past.

As we go through the AI revolution, cleaning up this historical record is doubly pressing—both to teach us where we come from, and to grant us the wisdom to build humanity's future.

The Opportunity

Today, new technologies give us the tools to solve these problems.

Blockchain and AI together can create a new standard for tagging artworks and heritage objects on a shared, transparent, immutable register.

For the first time, full records of provenance—ideally from find‑spot to current state—become recordable and verifiable.

The downstream unlocks would include:

  • Scholarship that can illuminate object histories and repair ruptures in the record
  • Institutional custodianship that is not vulnerable to legal claims
  • Workable cross‑border export controls
  • A functional marketplace that allows collectors to live with pieces of the past
The Conference

The convening should achieve six objectives for AABC.

01

Showcase AABC's research

Synthesize the AABC questionnaire and Bocconi case‑study findings into a public‑facing centerpiece.

02

Convene the people who matter

Bring together museums, scholars, governments, technologists, & patrons to collectively attack the problem.

03

Attract anchor funders

Use the event to build partnerships with tech companies and philanthropists, or else relationships that can lead to support.

04

Establish AABC as an authority on provenance

Be the mediator between art and tech on questions of authenticity, ownership, and the historical record.

05

Clarify AABC's next chapter

Resolve a strategic question: is AABC a consulting firm—McKinsey‑style mediator—or a platform builder?

06

Produce lasting outputs

Recorded talks, published proceedings, & downloadable case studies would serve as a foundation for the next stage of work.

Phasing & Scope

Proposed draft roadmap, phased over 16–18 weeks.

01 Discovery
4 weeks · $25,000
Activities
  • Conversations with all AABC stakeholders
  • Review of all documents and materials
  • Compiling of current institutional, tech, & other relevant relationships
  • Survey of comparable symposiums: structures, content, sustaining models
  • Mapping potential funders, partners, & venues
  • Identification of production vendors
Output
  • Symposium structure
  • Fundraising & GTM strategy
  • Pitch deck for funders, partners, and venues
  • Budget
02 Build
8 weeks · $50,000
Activities
  • Work with Susan and other key stakeholders to secure funders & partners
  • Confirm venue, dates, and production vendor
  • Lock in speakers, moderators, and panelists
  • Build content plan for pre-event content, multimedia capture, & post-event distribution
  • Set audience-curation and invitation strategy
  • Develop day-of production plan
Output
  • Speaker, sponsor, & venue program
  • Live microsite and registration
  • Communications campaign in motion
  • Production-ready run-of-show
03 Execute
4–6 weeks · $25,000
Activities
  • Oversee pre-event editorial & communications campaign
  • Work with vendor to manage day-of production
  • Host event and moderate as required
  • Ensure sponsor & partner obligations are met
  • Oversee media capture on the day: filming, photography, on-the-record interviews
  • Oversee media editing & distribution
Output
  • The symposium itself
  • A library of distributable media artifacts
  • Documented outcomes for funders and partners
  • A framework for the next iteration
Total: $100,000 across 16–18 weeks. Production budget—venue, AV, catering, travel, honoraria, and media-capture vendors—contracted separately by AABC with the event producer.
